First Conditional

Talk about possible situations in the present or future and their results.

📖 Grammar lesson

We use the first conditional to talk about a possible condition and its probable result in the future.

If + conditionPresent simple
Comma,
Main clausewill / modal verb / imperative
Structure: If + present simple, + will + infinitive.
If you study, you will pass the exam.
If he doesn't call, you should tell me immediately.
If your room is tidy, you can leave.

Remember: Do not use will after if in the usual first conditional structure.

🔎 Quick reminder

  • Condition: present simple — If it rains tomorrow...
  • Result: will + infinitive — ...we will stay at home.
  • Modal verbs: can, should, may, etc. — If she needs help, you should call her.
  • Imperative: Give an instruction or advice — If you feel tired, take a break.

You can put the if-clause first or second. Use a comma when the sentence starts with if.
